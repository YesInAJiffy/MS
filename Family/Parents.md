## Part 1: Is YouTube "Unlisted" a Good Idea?

An **Unlisted** video on YouTube means it won't show up in search results, on your channel, or in recommendations. Only someone with the exact link can watch it.

### The Pros:

* **The absolute easiest way to share:** You can text or email a link to relatives anywhere in the world, and they can watch it instantly on their phones, tablets, or TVs without downloading massive files.
* **Free, unlimited storage:** Life histories mean hours of high-definition footage, which would quickly fill up paid cloud storage like Google Drive or iCloud.

### The Cons & Risks:

* **"Security by Obscurity":** Unlisted is *not* password-protected. If a family member accidentally shares the link, or forwards an email containing it, anyone who clicks that link can view the video.
* **Copyright flags:** If your parents are reminiscing and there is a radio playing old film music in the background, YouTube’s automated system might flag the video for copyright. While an unlisted video won't get your account banned for this, YouTube might block the audio or place ads on your private family video.
* **No true ownership:** You are subject to YouTube's terms of service. If their automated system misinterprets something in a private video, it could be taken down.

### 💡 The Ultimate Recommendation: Use a "Dual-Backup" Strategy

Go ahead and use YouTube Unlisted for easy family viewing, but **do not let it be your only copy**. Keep a master copy of the raw video files on a physical external hard drive or a secure cloud service like Google Drive.

## Part 2: How to Organize the Collection

Since your parents are with you for 2–3 months, don't try to sit them down for one massive, exhausting 5-hour interview. Instead, break it into **short, 30-to-45-minute casual weekly sessions**.

To keep the collection organized and easy to navigate on YouTube, structure it into a cohesive timeline using **Playlists** and **Video Chapters**.

### 1. Structure by Era (The Playlists)

Create a Master Playlist on YouTube titled *"The Lifelong Journey of [Parents' Names]"*. Within that, organize the chapters chronologically:

* **Volume 1: Foundations & Childhood:** Growing up, their hometowns, family dynamics, school memories, and ancestral stories.
* **Volume 2: Adulthood, Love, & Career:** How they met, their early marriage years, navigating their careers, and the challenges of building a household.
* **Volume 3: Parenthood & Turning Points:** Raising their children (including your own childhood stories from their perspective!), major life moves, and family traditions.
* **Volume 4: Wisdom & Reflection:** Their philosophy on life, lessons learned, and their hopes for their grandchildren.

### 2. Use YouTube "Time Stamps" (Chapters)

When you upload the videos, you can use the description box to create a clickable index. This is incredibly helpful so that years from now, your kids can jump straight to a specific memory.

If you type timestamps into the YouTube description box exactly like this, YouTube will automatically chop the video player into clean, labeled chapters:

```text
00:00 - Introduction & Welcome
04:15 - Stories about grandfather's old house
12:40 - Moving to the city for university
22:15 - The day they first met

```

### 3. Quick Tips for the Sessions

* **Use Props:** Sit them at a table with old family photographs, heirlooms, or passports. Holding a physical object unlocks memories that general questions can't reach.
* **Focus on the "Why" and "How":** Don't just ask *where* they lived. Ask, *"What did the morning smell like in your childhood home?"* or *"How did you feel when you moved away from your hometown for the first time?"*
* **Get the Grandkids Involved:** Have your kids sit in on a session to ask one or two questions. Capturing three generations interacting on camera is pure magic.

Enjoy this time with them. The process of recording these stories will be just as memorable as the final collection itself.

If they give a short or vague answer to a question, use the **"Magic Follow-Up."** Simply pause, smile, and ask: *"What did that feel like?"* or *"Can you tell me a specific story about a time that happened?"* The best material always comes from the unscripted stories triggered by the follow-up.



# Life Story Interview Questions — For Mom & Dad

A ready-to-use question bank, organized by life stage. Pick 8–12 questions per session (20–40 min each) rather than trying to cover everything at once. Let tangents happen — that's usually where the best stories live.

---

## 1. Childhood (Ages ~0–12)

1. Where were you born, and what was that place like back then?
2. What do you remember about the house you grew up in? Can you describe each room?
3. Who was in your household growing up — siblings, grandparents, extended family?
4. What did your parents do for work? What were they like as people?
5. What's your earliest memory?
6. What games did you play as a child? Who did you play with?
7. What was school like? Who was your favorite or most memorable teacher?
8. Were you a shy kid or an outgoing kid? Were you naughty or well-behaved?
9. What was a typical day like for you at age 8 or 9?
10. Did your family have any traditions or rituals you looked forward to?
11. What's the most trouble you ever got into as a child?
12. Was there a family recipe or meal you remember most from childhood?
13. What did your neighborhood or village look like? Who were your neighbors?
14. Did you have any pets? Tell me about them.
15. What were you afraid of as a child?

---

## 2. Youth & Adolescence (Teens)

1. What were you like as a teenager?
2. What did you want to be when you grew up?
3. Who was your best friend, and what did you two get up to?
4. What music, movies, or fashion were popular when you were a teenager?
5. Did you have any hobbies or passions during this time?
6. What was the toughest part of being a teenager back then?
7. Did you have a first crush or first love? What happened?
8. Were there any big historical or world events happening that you remember clearly?
9. What rules did your parents have that you disagreed with?
10. Did you rebel in any way, big or small?
11. What did you do for fun with no money?
12. Was there a teacher, coach, or mentor who changed your path?

---

## 3. Education & Coming of Age

1. What was higher education (college/university/trade school) like for you, if you attended?
2. Why did you choose that path — was it your choice or expected of you?
3. What subject were you best at? What did you struggle with?
4. Did you work while studying? What was that job like?
5. What was the hardest decision you had to make in your early twenties?
6. Who influenced you most during this period of your life?
7. What did independence look like for you — when did you first live away from your parents?
8. What did you imagine your adult life would look like at this age? How close were you?

---

## 4. Marriage & Partnership

1. How did you and (Mom/Dad) meet? Tell me the whole story.
2. What was your first impression of each other?
3. What made you decide this was the person you wanted to marry?
4. Tell me about the proposal, or how the decision to marry came about.
5. What was your wedding day like?
6. Was your marriage arranged, or a personal choice — and what was that experience like?
7. What did your parents think of the match?
8. What were the early years of marriage like — the good and the hard parts?
9. What's something you learned about marriage that surprised you?
10. What advice would you give someone about choosing a life partner?

---

## 5. Career & Working Life

1. What was your first job? What did it pay, and what did that feel like?
2. How did you end up in the career/profession you had?
3. What was a typical work day like for most of your career?
4. What achievement from your working life are you proudest of?
5. Did you ever face a major setback, layoff, or failure at work? How did you get through it?
6. Who was the best boss or colleague you ever had — and the worst?
7. Did your work ever require sacrifices for the family? What were they?
8. If you could have chosen any other career, what would it have been?
9. What do you wish you had known about work/money when you were starting out?

---

## 6. Raising a Family / Parenting

1. What was it like when you found out you were going to be a parent for the first time?
2. What's your strongest memory from when I (or my siblings) were babies?
3. What was the hardest part of raising children?
4. What parenting philosophy did you try to follow — and where did it come from?
5. Did you feel you were repeating your own parents' approach, or doing it differently?
6. What's a memory with me/us that you cherish most?
7. Was there a time you were really scared for one of us?
8. What did you want most for your children's future?
9. What do you think you got right as a parent? What would you have done differently?
10. What's something about our childhood I probably don't remember but you do?

4. Did anyone in the family serve in the military or live through conflict? What did they share about it?
5. What's the biggest change you've seen in the world during your lifetime?
6. What technology amazed you the most as it emerged (television, telephone, internet, mobile phones)?
7. How has your hometown/country changed since you were young?

1. What values were most important to you throughout your life?

3. What's the best decision you ever made?

## 10. Photo-Narration Session (Separate Sitting)

Go through an old photo album on camera and ask, for each photo:
1. Who is in this photo, and where was it taken?
2. What was happening that day?
3. How old were you here, and what was going on in your life at the time?
4. Is anyone in this photo no longer with us? What do you remember about them?
5. Does this photo remind you of anything else?

---

## 11. Closing / Legacy Questions (Save for a Later Session)

1. What do you hope for the family in the next generation?
2. Is there anything you've never told us that you want us to know?
3. What's a piece of wisdom you want to pass down?
4. If you were writing a letter to your great-grandchildren, what would you say?
5. What's something about you that people don't know but you wish they did?

---

### Tips for the sessions
- Ask one question, then stay quiet — let silence sit, it often prompts more detail.
- Follow up with "what happened next?" and "how did that feel?" rather than moving to the next question too fast.
- It's fine to revisit a topic in a later session if something new comes up.
- Keep a running list of names/places mentioned so you can ask for spellings later — useful for captions and family trees.
