
### The Best Way to Structure a Twice-a-Month Routine

Instead of chasing a "lowest price day" that doesn't reliably exist, you should structure your twice-a-month routing based on **cash flow and psychological ease**.

Since Fidelity allows you to set precise dates for recurring investments, a highly effective setup is to schedule your transfers **in the middle of the calendar blocks (away from the turn-of-the-month spike)**:

* **Investment 1: The 10th of the month**
* **Investment 2: The 20th of the month**


Automating your finances takes the brain damage out of investing. Since you already have an AMEX High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA), you are actually in a fantastic position to get this done immediately.

Here is the exact step-by-step to automate everything in Fidelity, followed by what to do with your AMEX account.


**For automated bi-weekly investments (twice a month), here’s the traditional historical guidance on lower/stronger periods.** Keep in mind: these are **average seasonal patterns** over long periods — not guarantees, and they’re small edges. For long-term investing, **consistency matters far more than perfect timing**. Don’t over-optimize; the goal is to automate and move on.

### 1. Best/Worst Days of the Week (Historically)
- **Mondays**: Often the **weakest** day on average (slightly negative or lowest returns). Weekend news can create selling pressure.
- **Wednesdays**: Tend to be among the **strongest** (highest average daily returns).
- **Fridays**: Frequently positive (high probability of green days), though not always the highest average gain.

**For buying (wanting relatively lower prices)**: Schedule around **Monday** or early in the week if possible.

### 2. Best/Worst Times in the Month
- **Turn-of-the-Month Effect** (strongest pattern): Markets have historically shown **higher returns** in the last few trading days of the month + first few trading days of the new month. This is due to payrolls, pension contributions, rebalancing, etc.
- Mid-month periods are often relatively weaker or flatter.

### Recommended Bi-Weekly Schedule
Since you want **twice a month**, here are practical, historically informed options (adjust to your payday):

- **Option 1 (Good for potential lower entry)**: Around the **1st–3rd** and **15th–17th** (or nearest business day).
  - Early month captures part of the turn-of-month strength window.
  - Mid-month is neutral/average.

- **Option 2 (Slight dip bias)**: **Monday** (or Tuesday) closest to the 1st and 15th of the month.
  - Leverages the weaker Monday tendency.

- **Simple & Effective**: Set it on your **paydays** (e.g., 1st and 15th, or every other Friday). This is what most people do successfully — it aligns with cash flow and is easy to automate in Fidelity. The seasonal edges are minor compared to the benefit of investing regularly over years.

**In Fidelity**: When setting recurring investments/transfers, choose specific dates (e.g., 1st and 15th) or frequencies like “twice monthly.” It will skip weekends/holidays automatically.

### Final Advice
- These patterns have weakened somewhat in recent decades due to efficient markets and algorithmic trading, but the **turn-of-the-month** and **Monday weakness** are still observable in long-term data.
- **Don’t stress perfection**. If the market is “high” on your scheduled day, you’re still buying at a better price than if you waited in cash. Missing strong days hurts more than buying on a slightly expensive day.
- Start the automation soon (even if not on the “ideal” day) so your money starts working.

## Part 1: Setting Up Automated Investing in Fidelity

Fidelity has an incredibly useful **"Recurring Investments"** tool that lets you pull money straight from your external checking account and buy stocks, mutual funds, or ETFs automatically in a single action.

### Before You Start:

Log into Fidelity, go to **Transfer**, select **Manage bank accounts**, and link your external checking account. It takes a couple of days to verify via micro-deposits if you haven't done it yet.

### The Automated Workflow:

1. **Navigate to the Tool:** Fidelity Main Menu.
Log into your dashboard. From the top navigation bar, click on **Trade**, and select **Recurring Investments** from the dropdown menu.


2. **Create a New Investment Plan:** Choose what to buy.
Click **Create New Plan**. Choose **Stocks/ETFs** and type in the ticker symbol you want (for example, **VTI** for the Total US Stock Market).


3. **Set Amount and Frequency:** Select amount & cadence.
Select the specific Fidelity brokerage account you want the shares to go into. Enter the exact dollar amount you want to invest each period (e.g., $1,000). Set the frequency to **Monthly** or **Every 2 Weeks**.


4. **Set the Funding Source:** Crucial for automation.
Toggle the option that says **"Use a linked bank account"**. Select your checking account. This tells Fidelity to automatically pull the cash from your bank on your scheduled day and buy the index fund immediately, meaning you never have cash sitting idle.


5. **Preview and Confirm:** Final review.
Review your dates and amounts, then click **Confirm**.

## Part 2: Should You Open Another HYSA?

**Short answer: No, you don't need to open another one.**

American Express offers a fantastic, top-tier HYSA backed by highly reliable customer service and ironclad stability. There is almost zero practical reason to open a second HYSA unless you fall into one of two exceptions:

1. **You exceed FDIC limits:** If you are planning to park more than **$250,000 in cash** purely in savings, you should split anything above that limit into a second bank (like Capital One, Marcus, or SoFi) to ensure 100% of your money is federally insured.
2. **You are chasing fractional returns:** While AMEX sometimes sits roughly 0.20% to 0.40% below the absolute highest, most aggressive online-only banks on the market, moving your money to chase a tiny fraction of a percent usually isn't worth the administrative headache of managing multiple logins and tax documents.

### Your Game Plan for the Checking Account Cash:

Instead of opening a new account, just use your existing AMEX HYSA to execute the **"Yield & Shield"** strategy we talked about:

* **Keep a baseline** in your checking account to cover the current month's bills and automated Fidelity pulls.
* **Transfer your Emergency Fund** (3–6 months of expenses) straight into your AMEX HYSA right now so it starts earning interest immediately.
* **If you have a large chunk of excess cash** that you want to invest but are too nervous to drop into the stock market all at once, move *that* into the AMEX HYSA too. You can then let your automated Fidelity plan slowly pull from your checking account over the next year, occasionally refilling checking from your AMEX balance as needed.

This gives you a clean, simplified two-bank system: **Checking** for monthly cash flow, **AMEX** for your safe cash shield, and **Fidelity** for your automated wealth engine.
